THE PROBLEM
-----------
It was not that the model would not think. It was that everything
about how it was asked told it not to.

Three things were doing that:

1. The brief was mostly evidence tables and closed with "use these
   figures exactly; do not add figures that are not here." That reads
   as an instruction to do analysis, not strategy.

2. The first and hardest rule was "never invent a number." Applied
   broadly it produced a defensive voice — hence "Nothing in the
   evidence touches infusion pumps. This is genuinely new ground."
   That was obedience, and it read as refusal.

3. Nothing ever asked for market knowledge. Not how this equipment
   gets bought, not who else competes, not when budgets appear. Never
   requested, so never offered.


WHAT CHANGED
------------

A new first pass: THE MARKET READ

Before it touches reLink's data at all, it now writes what it knows
about the category:

  - How this actually gets bought: who raises it, who specifies, who
    signs, how long approval takes, and whether the money is capital,
    operating, or a disposal that funds itself
  - What triggers it: service line closures, fleet standardisation,
    OR refits, mergers, OEM end-of-life notices, fiscal year end —
    and when in the year they cluster
  - Where these buyers already are: the searches, the marketplaces
    and auction sites, the forums, the named competitors who show up,
    and the gap reLink can stand in
  - What usually works in this category and what reliably does not
  - Likely economics, marked as market judgement, with what to verify
    in Keyword Planner first

Everything after it builds on that. The idea pass is told outright:
if the idea would work equally well for any company selling any
equipment, it is not the idea.

Rules rewritten

  Before: never invent a number, full stop
  Now:    figures attributed to reLink must come from the brief;
          market norms, competitor behaviour, category benchmarks and
          cost expectations are open, and should be brought freely,
          marked as market judgement so a reader can tell them apart

Plus an explicit ban on the thing that was annoying you: never open a
section by describing what the evidence lacks. If there is no
precedent, make the call, label it a hypothesis, give the two-week
test that would kill it.

The brief reframed

The evidence section is now titled "Reference data" and introduced as
a check on thinking rather than the boundary of it. The closing line
changed from forbidding new figures to inviting judgement.

WHAT TO JUDGE IT ON
-------------------
Run the same infusion pump brief and read the market read first. If it
names real competitors, real buying triggers and real timing, the
change worked. If it is generic B2B filler that would fit any product,
tell me and the fix is more specificity in that pass, not more data.

Worth knowing: this is now the model's knowledge of the medical
equipment market, which is broad but not current and not reLink's.
Treat the market read as a well-read outsider's opinion — useful,
occasionally wrong, and worth your correction where it is.


NEXT STEP IF YOU WANT IT
------------------------
The API supports giving the model live web search, so the market read
could cite current competitor pricing and real listings rather than
working from memory. That is a genuine step up and it is a contained
change — one stage, one tool definition. It costs more per run and
adds time, so it is worth deciding deliberately rather than by
default. Say the word.
