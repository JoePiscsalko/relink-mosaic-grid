CAMPAIGN BUILDER — strategy, not a keyword report
==================================================

Two files, both replacing what is there:

    src/CampaignBuilder.jsx
    netlify/functions/campaign.js

Add file -> Upload files, drag in BOTH folders (src and netlify),
commit. No new environment variables. No hand edits.


WHAT CHANGED, AND WHY
----------------------

1. The plan is a campaign now, not a keyword audit.

The old prompt led with performance data, so the model treated the
whole job as an analytics exercise with some copy stapled on. That
was a fault in how it was asked, not in what it can do.

It now writes:

  The idea             the concept, the angle, a working name, and
                       why it lands now
  Who you're talking to the buying situation, who signs off vs who
                       searches, the objections that kill the deal
  The offer            what makes someone act now, options with
                       trade-offs, and a recommendation
  Channel plan         sequenced week by week, then real depth per
                       channel:
                         Search and SEO — intent, the page to rank,
                           what to build
                         Google Ads — structure, budget, what to turn
                           off to fund it
                         LinkedIn — audience, size check, creative
                           concept, three-touch sequence
                         Email — where it sits in the journeys, a
                           send-by-send flow, segmentation
                         Organic social and sales enablement
  Copy                 RSAs with character counts, LinkedIn variants
                       matching the sequence, subject lines, social
                       posts — written to ship
  Keywords             one section now, not the whole plan
  Landing page         slug, H1, sections, CTA, proof, FAQs, internal
                       links
  How you'll know      the numbers to watch, what good looks like, a
                       two-week checkpoint and a stop condition
  SFMC email           complete HTML block

The no-invented-numbers rule still holds, and is now explicitly
scoped to numbers. Ideas, angles and tactics it generates freely.

Output limit raised from 8,000 to 16,000 tokens to fit all of it.
Cost per run goes from roughly 6 cents to roughly 12.


2. Keyword evidence no longer depends on publishing.

The builder read keywords from the last published dataset. A
"Replace everything" import clears those, which is why the panel kept
saying "No keyword data loaded" — and a campaign plan was about to be
generated against zero performance history without flagging it.

The 182 keywords already in App.jsx are now bundled into the builder
as a fallback. Published data still wins; this only fills the gap.
Nothing new is exposed — same data, same public repo, one file over.

Importing a fresh Google Ads keyword report still overrides it, and
is still worth doing.


3. The demand matcher was producing nonsense.

"Removal of surgery equipment" was being split into removal, surgery,
equipment — and "equipment" appears in nearly every path label you
have. It matched 254 of 307 leads and presented that as demand
evidence for surgical removal specifically.

Generic words are now ignored: equipment, medical, device, system,
unit, hospital, product and so on. The evidence panel also shows
which words it actually matched on, so a surprising number is
traceable rather than mysterious.

If a focus contains only generic words the panel says so instead of
quietly matching everything.


WORTH KNOWING
-------------
More output means more surface for a confident wrong statement. The
figures are constrained to the brief, but the strategy is the model's
own thinking and deserves the same read you would give a new agency's
first deck: good ideas in there, and some that do not survive contact
with what you know about the business.

The LinkedIn audience-size warning is still hard-coded, so every plan
has to state a minimum viable audience before recommending spend.
